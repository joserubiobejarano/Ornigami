**Decision date: October 4, 2026. Status: owner-approved direction and delegated defaults; implementation/acceptance still required.** The owner said the audience is not yet available, likely beauty salons, restaurants and gyms over the next few weeks; customers always pay and only the demo is free; Jose handles operations; privacy/performance choices are delegated; launch direction is approved. ## Commercial scope and rollout

Start with Review Booster, email only, one business/location and a validated manual Google review URL. Recruit up to **five businesses**, opening first to one, then three, then five after at least seven consecutive healthy operating days at each stage. Review the rollout after **30 days from the first paying customer's activation**. These are operational review periods, not free trials or subscription end dates. Named businesses and the actual start date remain to be supplied when recruited; no deadline is invented.

Use the existing Review Booster catalog: **EUR 39/month or EUR 360/year**, 500 accepted requests per full UTC calendar month, one location/user. Display billing interval, taxes as applicable and cancellation terms before checkout. No discount, free production entitlement, waived first payment or informal manual active-plan grant. The demo is the only free product experience. Do not sell Replies or Complete in this initial offer while Google acceptance is blocked; their catalog prices are not changed by this policy.

**New production subscriptions have no free trial.** Payment is collected before activation and entitlements follow authoritative successful billing reconciliation, not the success-page redirect or an abandoned checkout. The October 3 one-time 14-day trial policy is historical and superseded for new customers. Preserve already issued commitments until their existing end; do not shorten an existing trial, silently charge a user or erase its audit history. Expire/reconcile any still-open legacy trial checkout paths before onboarding under the new offer. Test pricing, checkout, portal changes/cancellation, failed/replayed/out-of-order webhooks and unchanged UTC quota ownership against the new contract.

The earlier Stripe acceptance skip is no longer compatible with the approved paid launch. Narrow Stripe acceptance is required. Start in an isolated Stripe test-mode target; this decision does not authorize a live charge, refund, customer email or change to an existing customer's subscription. A controlled live payment needs an identified authorized payer/payment method before execution. One recorded test and deployment acceptance closes each criterion; do not repeat unrelated mail/auth/provider packages.

## Mail, privacy and support defaults

Treat review requests as **optional customer communications requiring explicit permission for this purpose** in the initial rollout, rather than assuming transactional status or relying on an existing-customer marketing exception. Participating businesses record the permission source, timestamp and notice version before upload/intake; a generic business attestation alone is not per-recipient proof. No purchased/scraped lists or reuse of a booking address as automatic permission. Provide business identity, a clear reason for the request, privacy information and an immediate free unsubscribe in every request. Account/security/billing mail remains a separate necessary service category and must not carry promotions. This conservative product choice follows the authorization and opt-out framework in [LSSI articles 21–22](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2002-13758#a21); it does not assert that every review request has the same legal classification.

Collect only contact email, optional first name, actual visit time, business/location and permission evidence needed for the request. For salons/gyms, exclude health conditions, treatment notes and other sensitive service details from intake, generated messages, logs and AI prompts. Neutral requests go to eligible consenting customers without filtering by expected satisfaction. No incentives or review-conversion claims inferred from clicks.

For business-customer mail, the business determines its recipient/purpose and Ornigami processes the data on its documented instructions. Ornigami is responsible for its own account, billing, security and suppression purposes. Provide a controller/processor agreement, current subprocessor/transfer information and accurate notices before importing real customer data; verify the arrangement for the actual jurisdiction. No new legal-compliance certification is inferred from choosing these defaults.

**Jose is the release, monitoring, support, privacy-request and rollback owner.** Route published privacy/support addresses to the existing private operator workflow; retain the published `privacy@ornigami.com` contact and prove delivery/routing before enrollment. Aim to acknowledge within one business day; handle rights requests within one calendar month, or communicate a permitted extension and reasons within that first month, as described by the [EDPB rights guide](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/be-compliant/respect-individuals-rights_en). Export remains available. Self-service account deletion and uncertain-provider recovery remain disabled until their distinct provider/race acceptance gates pass; Jose needs a documented safe assisted-request process that respects those deadlines rather than promising immediate provider erasure.

## Retention defaults and required implementation

These are chosen product defaults, **not a claim that cleanup already enforces them**. Retention follows purpose, minimization and documented exceptions, consistent with the [EDPB data-protection principles](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/be-compliant/be-compliant_en). A UUID or keyed hash can still be personal data. Apply shorter valid erasure requests where applicable; retain only a specifically documented legal obligation/claim hold. Never silently purge operational fences or reactivate a suppressed address.

| Data class | Chosen limit / start point | Purpose and safeguards |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary Booster contact/visit and generated mail content | 90 days after terminal visit/delivery outcome | Short support window. Minimize earlier where possible; pending/unknown outcomes are excluded from automatic purge until resolved. Keep minimal ledger keys separately. |
| Optional/raw delivery diagnostics | Do not collect unnecessary provider payloads; any necessary raw diagnostic expires after 30 days | No message body/address copied into observability. |
| Normalized delivery events, provider correlation, idempotency/usage evidence | 365 days from terminal outcome, then purge linkable identifiers after safe replay/fence checks | One-year delivery/reconciliation support; aggregate non-identifying counts may remain. Late callbacks after disposal must be safely acknowledged without new delivery/quota effects. |
| Review-link clicks and integration event details | 90 days from event | Reduce existing 365-day detail retention; retain only minimal intake dedupe keys for 365 days where necessary to prevent replay. Neither clicks nor events become review attribution. |
| Permission evidence | While permission is relied on; minimal restricted record for 365 days after the last request or withdrawal | Proof of authorized sending; retain only source/time/notice version and necessary recipient reference. A documented longer legal hold is separate. |
| Unsubscribe, complaint and bounce suppression | Minimal do-not-send evidence while that address/objection remains relevant to sending; necessity reviewed at least annually | No arbitrary 90/365-day expiry that would resume mail. Separate from ordinary customer records; limit access/use to suppression. Remove only after a verified lawful withdrawal/change or cessation of the sending purpose; annual review is not automatic resubscription. |
| Legacy trial-owner/business anti-abuse evidence | Minimize after issued trials finish; purge 180 days after final legacy trial closure/reconciliation once all new-trial entry paths are disabled | No perpetual one-time-trial history for an offer no longer available. If a legacy promise still needs evidence, document its hold; do not reset eligibility by deleting rows while trial entry remains enabled. |
| Resolved deletion/provider-operation identifiers and erasure receipts | 365 days after verified completion | Restricted completion evidence; receipt does not prove all provider financial records were erased. Unresolved work is fenced, reviewed by Jose every 30 days and minimized as resolution allows. No indefinite hold without a recorded reason/next review. |
| Account/workspace configuration | While service is used; 90-day export/recovery window after termination, then erase unless a specific exception applies | Valid erasure requests follow the rights process, not a compulsory 90-day wait. Keep paid operations disabled after termination. |
| Accounting/invoice records | Where Spanish commercial record rules apply, six years from the last relevant accounting entry; adjust for documented applicable obligations | Restricted financial archive, not six-year retention of customer review lists, OAuth secrets or all Stripe payloads. [Commercial Code article 30](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1885-6627#art30). Confirm seller/jurisdiction and provider retention disclosures before paid onboarding. |
| Existing leads/demo events, feedback, cron/rate-limit and expired auth records | Keep existing implemented windows: 90 days leads/demo, 365 feedback, 30 cron, 2 rate-limit; expired token cleanup as implemented | No unrelated legacy-module reopening. Target rolling application backups at no more than 35 days; inventory actual vendor/PITR/log retention, exceptions and access before enrollment. Reapply erasure/suppression on restore; do not claim a vendor deletes within this target without verified configuration. |

Implementation must separate payload minimization from durable idempotency/usage/lifecycle keys, establish terminal timestamps and foreign-key effects, and retain unresolved operations without blind retries. Use bounded daily cleanup, private dry-run counts and disposable database tests before activation. Public notices change with the deployed behavior; do not advertise these target periods as already enforced. No destructive production cleanup is authorized by this document alone.

## Performance and capacity defaults

Accept the existing four-page owner navigation and twelve authenticated HTTP samples as a **preliminary production baseline**, with their recorded limits. They establish neither populated customer capacity nor browser CWV. Initial scope is at most five Booster businesses, one location/user each: **2,500 total plan requests per UTC month** at full individual allowances. Do not introduce undisclosed daily caps or lower the paid 500-request allowance. Any processing safety bound must fairly continue eligible work without changing quota or the existing 23-hour-to-seven-day send eligibility.

| Metric | Chosen target | Acceptance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Authenticated page HTML response | Per-route median <=2 s and p95 <=3 s | At least 30 read-only samples per route from a recorded location/network; report cold/warm and failures. Existing three samples/route do not establish p95. |
| Representative read load | Ten concurrent authenticated read sessions, p95 <=3 s and no unexpected 5xx/timeouts | Disposable isolated production-build target with business-scoped synthetic volumes (reuse existing large-data fixtures); no production load test, copied customer data or external side effects. |
| Browser experience | LCP <=2.5 s, INP <=200 ms, CLS <=0.1 at field p75, separately mobile/desktop | These [Google thresholds](https://web.dev/articles/defining-core-web-vitals-thresholds) are quality targets. Lab/mobile evidence before scale, field confirmation when real visits exist; do not label CLI timings or Lighthouse as field p75. Lack of field traffic alone does not block the first small cohort. Use an authorized measurement surface. |
| Availability | Internal goal 99.5% over a calendar month; report actual request/monitor denominators | Not a contractual SLA or an existing measured pass; low-volume/no-data is explicitly reported. |
| Sending/operations | Zero duplicate sends, suppression/tenancy bypass or unaccounted quota; natural jobs/health healthy | Existing fences, fairness, cron budgets/alerts remain. Provider acceptance is distinct from delivered mail and reviews. |

Before the first paying business: close paid-checkout acceptance, permission/notice/support-routing and retention implementation, representative read-load acceptance and fresh naturally scheduled health proof. Complete the larger HTTP sample and authorized mobile lab measurement before growing beyond the first business. Review operational/performance results daily in week one and weekly afterward. Pause new enrollment immediately for data isolation/suppression/duplicate-send defects, unhealthy/missing scheduled jobs or unconfirmed alert transport. Investigate a per-route p95 above 3 seconds on two consecutive adequately sampled windows; hold expansion until corrected. Preserve unknown-provider fences during rollback.
